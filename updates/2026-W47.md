## What's new in GEO

### Publisher controls & standards

- **[IETF] An individual draft, `draft-meunier-webbotauth-capacity-management-00` ("PACE"), proposes combining Web Bot Auth with the MOLE architecture so that an origin can give separate budgets to the clients behind one shared agent platform, without needing a stable client identifier.** It is an individual Informational draft by two Cloudflare authors, not a working-group document or an RFC. ([IETF datatracker, 15 Nov 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-meunier-webbotauth-capacity-management/))
  - Why it matters: it points to rate and budget controls for AI agent traffic that work per client rather than per platform, beyond allowing or blocking a whole operator.
- **[IETF] An individual draft, `draft-arsentev-crawler-traffic-reporting-00`, proposes that crawler traffic reports count requests per token by verification level (declared, route-consistent, verified, unverifiable) and defines a JSON report object for this.** It points to robots.txt (RFC 9309) and the webbotauth HTTP Message Signatures work (RFC 9421) as related work, does not define how to verify a crawler, and leaves access policy out of scope. It is an individual Informational draft, not a working-group document. ([IETF datatracker, 15 Nov 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-arsentev-crawler-traffic-reporting/))
  - Why it matters: AI crawler statistics keyed only on the User-Agent header count what clients declare, not what can be verified. This draft proposes a shared way to report the difference.

### Google documentation

- **[Google] Search Central adds a documentation page on identifying traffic from Google Search in analytics and server logs through the HTTP Referer header.** Search uses an origin referrer policy, so only the scheme and host are sent. The page does not separate AI Overviews or AI Mode visits from other Search visits. ([Google Search Central documentation updates, entry 12 Nov 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/updates); [the page, last updated 12 Nov 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/identify-google-search-traffic))
  - Why it matters: it is the primary reference for attributing Search referrals in logs, and it gives no way to tell clicks from Google's AI features apart from other Search clicks.
- **[Google] A Search Central blog post on moving a site to a CDN recommends exempting verified crawlers from bot blocking (for example, by Google's published IP ranges), warming the cache before the cutover, and briefly returning 429 or 503 if the origin is overloaded.** ([Google Search Central blog, 11 Nov 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/11/crawling-cdn))
  - Why it matters: CDN and firewall bot rules that catch verified crawlers stop Googlebot from fetching pages. The post is about crawling in general and does not mention AI features or `Google-Extended`.

### AI answer engines

- **[OpenAI] OpenAI's official news feed lists "Businesses in ChatGPT", described as a way for businesses to "[m]anage your business information, get insights about how your business shows up, and turn interest into action in ChatGPT".** ([OpenAI news RSS feed, 10 Nov 2026](https://openai.com/news/rss.xml))
  - Why it matters: OpenAI describes it as an official way for a business to manage its information in ChatGPT and see insights about how it shows up there. The announcement page could not be opened on 2026-11-16, so eligibility, regions, data sources and how the insights are measured are not asserted here (see *Watching*).
- **[Perplexity] Perplexity releases Decider v1.3 and Decider Flash v1.3, decision models that return probabilities for classification, routing and scoring.** Among its internal uses, the post says Perplexity can use Decider during crawling to judge page quality so that low-quality pages (error pages, anti-bot walls, spam, SEO filler, navigation-only pages) are filtered out of its index. It also reports reranking benchmark results. ([Perplexity blog, 12 Nov 2026](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider))
  - Why it matters: the vendor says a model-based quality judgement during crawling is used to filter pages out of its index, upstream of any citation. The post does not disclose the quality criteria or how cited sources are chosen.

### Research

- **[arXiv preprint] "Lost in Delegation" compares ways in which an LLM orchestrator in generative search hands tasks to tools or to other LLMs, on datasets split before and after the knowledge cutoff.** It reports no consistent accuracy gain from delegation, finds that delegation can substantially degrade accuracy when the task needs information the orchestrator lacks, and reports better results from direct tool use. ([arXiv:2611.16526, submitted 12 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16526))
  - Why it matters: this is adjacent to GEO rather than core. Delegated queries can be distorted or lose information, which changes what content is retrieved. The paper does not study citation, visibility or named commercial engines.

### Industry studies

- **[Ahrefs — industry study] Across 5,516,381 US ChatGPT answers in its Brand Radar index (all topics, November 2026), Ahrefs finds Walmart named in 95,605 answers (1.73%) and Target in 52,096 (0.94%).** Stores take six of the top 10 places and carmakers 15 of the top 100. Ahrefs describes the questions as a large set of real, search-backed questions and gives no prompt count or exact dates. The data is US-only and ChatGPT-only, comes from Ahrefs' own product, and the brand list was cleaned with stated rules (aliases merged, sub-brands kept separate, ambiguous names excluded after reading sample answers). ([Ahrefs, 11 Nov 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/most-mentioned-brands-chatgpt/))
  - Why it matters: it is a large-sample baseline for brand share of answers. Even the most-named brand appears in under 2% of answers when the base covers every topic.
- **[SparkToro — industry study] Using Similarweb panel data for 2021–2026, SparkToro reports that worldwide traffic to the top million sites (which SparkToro calls the open web) peaked in Q1 2022 at 2.243 trillion visits and was 12.2% below that peak in Q3 2026 (1.970 trillion), while visits per web user fell 29.2% as users grew from 4.95 billion to 6.15 billion.** The data covers mobile and desktop web only, with apps excluded, and user counts come from DataReportal. The author says the causes are not confirmed and names AI tools as only one possible factor. The article's URL says "24%", while the body's figures are 12.2% for Q3 2026 and 14.7% at the Q1 2026 low. ([SparkToro, 10 Nov 2026](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-traffic-to-the-open-web-has-fallen-24-in-5-years/))
  - Why it matters: it is macro context for zero-click and AI-answer discussions, read with its stated limits.

### Prompting

- **[Anthropic] With a model launch on 9 Nov 2026, Anthropic publishes a model-specific prompting guide covering effort calibration, preventing early stopping, progress updates, delegation to subagents, and reviewing prompts written for earlier models.** The guide page prints no date. The date comes from the Claude Platform release notes. ([Claude Platform release notes, entry 9 Nov 2026](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview); [the guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-2))
  - Why it matters: vendor guidance notes that some instructions written for earlier models no longer work as intended, so stored prompts, including those used for GEO measurement, are worth re-testing when the model changes. Nothing in the guide is specific to web search or citations.
