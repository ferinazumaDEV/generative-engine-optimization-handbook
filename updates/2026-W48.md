## What's new in GEO

### Publisher controls & standards

- **[IETF] An individual draft, `draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal-01`, proposes expressing a "no AI training" preference in robots.txt as an ordinary group whose `User-Agent` line carries a policy token, `__usage_ai-training`, with no new directive and no change to the robots.txt grammar.** The author's affiliation is Anthropic. It is an individual draft with no stream defined, not a working-group document, and it covers training only. ([IETF datatracker, 16 Nov 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/); [draft text](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal-01.txt))
  - Why it matters: it is a robots.txt-native proposal that sits beside the `Content-Usage` approach in the working group's attachment draft (the comparison is this handbook's reading, not the draft's), so the syntax publishers will end up writing for AI-training preferences is still unsettled. The draft does not say which crawlers would honour the token.
- **[IETF] The AIPREF working group had two sessions on the IETF 127 agenda, on 16 Nov and 19 Nov 2026.** The agenda lists attachment-draft issues for the first session (including whether the path in `Content-Usage` in robots.txt should be part of a structured field) and vocabulary-draft issues for the second (the definition of a generative AI model, distinguishing access from use, and caching, embeddings and derived forms). No minutes were posted as of 2026-11-23, so no outcome is asserted here (see *Watching*). ([IETF datatracker, agenda last updated 15 Nov 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/agenda-127-aipref/))
  - Why it matters: an agenda is a plan, not a decision. Core vocabulary questions are still open issues, so neither working-group draft should be described as final.
- **[IETF] The webbotauth working group met at IETF 127, and its posted minutes (version `-00`) report a review of the adopted `draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol` and state that "tracking or assigning reputation to individual users or sub-agent sessions remains out of scope under the current charter".** The minutes are a summary and record no new adoption in the sections read. ([IETF datatracker, session dated 18 Nov 2026 UTC](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/minutes-127-webbotauth/))
  - Why it matters: bot-identity standardisation currently has one adopted protocol document, and its scope is held to identifying the bot and its operator, not the user behind it.
- **[IETF] An individual draft, `draft-suresh-webbotauth-verification-outcome-00`, proposes a problem type and reason codes so that a verifier can tell a signed bot which Web Bot Auth outcome it reached (verified, invalid or unverified) and why.** It is a single-author `-00` individual draft, not a working-group document. ([IETF datatracker, 21 Nov 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-suresh-webbotauth-verification-outcome/))
  - Why it matters: it names a gap in Web Bot Auth as specified today, where, in the draft's words, "[a] client whose request is refused cannot tell a bad signature from an expired one, or a missing key from a directory the verifier could not fetch".

### Google documentation

- **[Google] Google's crawling documentation adds a guide, "Allow Google-Agent traffic", on letting Google-Agent through a CDN, firewall or bot-detection service.** It tells site owners to check that the `User-Agent` contains `Google-Agent`, that the source IP is in `user-triggered-agents.json`, and, where present, the Web Bot Auth signature for the `agent.bot.goog` identity. Google says it "is also experimenting with the Web Bot Auth protocol". ([Google crawling changelog, entry 17 Nov 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/changelog); [the guide, last updated 17 Nov 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/allow-google-agent))
  - Why it matters: it is a first-party recipe for not blocking agent traffic, and it documents HTTP message signatures as one verification path. Google-Agent is a user-triggered agent, not a search or training crawler, and the guide says nothing about AI Overviews or AI Mode retrieval. Signing is described as an experiment, not a requirement.
- **[Google] The Search Analytics API now supports the branded-queries classification (`brandedClassification`), the web multimodal search type (`type=webMultimodal`: Google Lens, Circle to Search and image uploads) and multi-country and multi-device filters (`filterLists`).** Branded data is only available for eligible properties; for other properties "the response won't include any rows". ([Google Search Central blog, 16 Nov 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/11/search-analytics-api-update))
  - Why it matters: branded versus non-branded reporting can now be automated. The post does not add AI Overviews or AI Mode data to the API, and an empty branded response means the property is not eligible, not that it has no branded traffic.

### AI answer engines

- **[Google] AI Mode gets "temporary threads": a session "completely detached from your account", whose responses are not informed by past preferences, do not affect future personalisation and are not saved to history.** Google says it is rolling out globally on desktop and mobile browsers. ([Google blog, 17 Nov 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/temporary-threads-ai-mode/))
  - Why it matters: it is a vendor-documented, account-detached condition for AI Mode, which is relevant as a control when observing which sources an answer cites. The post says nothing about citations or sources, or about signals that do not depend on the account, such as location.

### Research

- **[arXiv preprint] "GEOHunter" measures one paid GEO content network on three Chinese user-generated-content platforms: 1,985 accounts and 466,357 inferred promotional placements.** The abstract reports that across nine generative engines from the US and China, "4.5% to 20% of retrievable cited pages are attributable to the network, depending on how strictly attribution is drawn", and that on eight of the engines the network's clients are named in 52% of answers against 68% for category leaders. ([arXiv:2611.26564, submitted 19 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26564))
  - Why it matters: it is an in-the-wild measurement of how much of an answer engine's citations a paid placement network can occupy. It covers one network, the placements are inferred, and the abstract does not name the engines, the query set or the denominators, and reports no causal test.

### Industry studies

- **[Semrush blog — industry study] A single-author, hand-run study of 480 local-intent Google searches (12 US markets, 10 services, signed out, September–November 2026) reports AI Overviews on 38% of searches in its first round and on 99% (79 of 80) in its last, where the AI Overview replaced the map pack.** The 99% figure comes from the last four markets only, and the author says that round was captured on a single day and "could be a test or a rollout that hasn't reached everyone yet". ([Semrush blog, 17 Nov 2026](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-are-taking-over-the-map-pack/))
  - Why it matters: it is small but denominated evidence that AI Overviews can appear on transactional local queries. It is one author's manual collection, US only, and it measures appearance, not clicks or calls.
- **[Seer Interactive — industry study] Seer ran 20 prompts through the OpenAI API on 4 Nov 2026 and read the model's reasoning summaries. It reports that fewer than 1 in 10 brands were named before the first search, and that searches aimed at one website (`site:` queries) were 13% of queries in the first search round and 75% in later rounds.** ([Seer Interactive, 18 Nov 2026](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/chatgpt-reasoning))
  - Why it matters: Seer reads it as the model checking a shortlist against individual sites after forming it. The sample is 20 prompts on one day through the API, not the ChatGPT app, and reasoning summaries are written by the model, not a log of the retrieval pipeline.
- **[SparkToro — industry study, Verso panel data] SparkToro analyses 118,163 prompts across 41,961 ChatGPT conversations from 12,321 US opt-in panel users (1 Jan – 29 Oct 2026, with 131 heavy users excluded).** The largest per-user categories it reports are images and visual design (16.1%) and information and entertainment (14.0%). ([SparkToro, 17 Nov 2026](https://sparktoro.com/blog/we-analyzed-11800-chatgpt-prompts-from-12000-users-to-answer-how-do-americans-use-ai-tools-in-2026/))
  - Why it matters: it is a denominated estimate of what ChatGPT is used for. The panel is opt-in, US and ChatGPT only, the data belongs to a third party, the shares are per user rather than per prompt, and the categories are the author's. This entry does not quote a share for commercial or product-research prompts.

### Prompting

- **[Google] The Gemini API release notes list Gemini 4 Neon (`gemini-4-neon`) as generally available on 18 Nov 2026, with four thinking levels (`low`, `medium`, `high` as the default, and `xhigh`) and a 1M-token context window.** ([Gemini API changelog, entry 18 Nov 2026](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog))
  - Why it matters: a new model with a new default thinking level changes the baseline that stored prompts, including those used for GEO measurement, are run against. The entries read for 17–19 Nov contain no change to prompting guidance for text models and nothing about grounding or citations.

### Gemini Notebook

- **[Google] A Google Workspace Updates post uses the product name "Gemini Notebook" and announces that it "can now turn sources into interactive sites".** The post gives a gradual rollout starting 16 Nov 2026, on the web, for users aged 18 and over. ([Google Workspace Updates, 18 Nov 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/transform-your-notes-and-research-into-dynamic-tools-with-Gemini-Notebook.html))
  - Why it matters: it is an official, dated use of the name "Gemini Notebook", which W47 could not source. The post does not announce or date a rename from NotebookLM, and says nothing about an API, source limits or quotas.

## Follow-ups from W47

- **[OpenAI] The "Introducing Businesses in ChatGPT" announcement, which could not be opened for W47, opened on 2026-11-23.** It describes a beta in which "[a]ny eligible business located where ChatGPT is available can claim their business" through a Business Hub, then, once verified, update key details and add product feeds with pricing and availability. Insights are aggregated: the number of mentions and website clicks, and "discovery topics" across conversations where the business's website is cited. Business Agents are limited to eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States. ([OpenAI, 10 Nov 2026](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt))
  - Why it matters: it is a first-party channel for supplying business facts to ChatGPT and a first-party count of mentions and clicks. The post does not say that claiming a business changes whether or how it is cited, does not define "eligible", and gives no figures for the thresholds above which insights are shown.
- **[arXiv preprint] "IntentGEO: Intent-aware Generative Engine Optimization for Recommendation" evaluates GEO rewrites across user intents and reports that the gains of existing GEO methods vary by intent.** Its proposed method, AMO, "improves average citation-rank gain by 35% over the strongest prior learning-based baseline" across four domains and ten intent states. ([arXiv:2611.04225, submitted 3 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04225))
  - Why it matters: it treats user intent as a dimension along which GEO gains do not transfer. The setting is LLM recommendation over item descriptions, not general web answer engines, and the 35% is relative to a baseline, with no absolute values in the abstract.
