## What's new in GEO

### Publisher controls & standards

- **[Cloudflare] Monetization Gateway opens in closed beta so sellers can charge AI clients through HTTP 402 Payment Required (the x402 protocol).** It is limited to eligible U.S.-based sellers and buyers, verification and settlement run through Coinbase's x402 Facilitator, and payments settle in USDC on the Base blockchain. ([Cloudflare, 30 Sep 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway-beta/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: this is a per-request payment rail at the HTTP layer, separate from Pay Per Use. Outside the U.S. it is not available yet.
- **[Cloudflare] "Redirects for AI Training" sends verified AI training crawlers a 301 to a page's canonical URL, while humans, search crawlers and AI Search fetchers see the original page.** It is switched on under AI Crawl Control > Quick Actions, on Pro, Business and Enterprise plans at no extra cost; on 2026-11-02 the changelog's newest entry is still 16 Jun 2026. ([Cloudflare AI Crawl Control changelog, entry 17 Apr 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/changelog/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: with this on, training crawlers and search crawlers can receive different responses for the same URL. Canonical tags now decide what a training crawler receives, so a wrong canonical sends it to the wrong page.
- **[IETF] The Web Bot Auth working group's protocol draft, `draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-00`, defines how automated HTTP clients sign their requests.** It defines a `Signature-Agent` header, a JWKS-based key directory and a well-known URI for that directory; its authors are from Cloudflare and Google, it is intended for the Standards Track, and it is a work in progress, not an RFC. ([IETF Internet-Draft, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-00.txt)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: bot verification by signature is moving into a standards track. It can complement IP and reverse-DNS checks but does not yet replace them.
- **[IETF] The AIPREF individual draft on display-based preferences has expired with no newer revision.** The datatracker lists `draft-madhavan-aipref-displaybasedpref-02` (latest revision 25 Mar 2026) as "Expired & archived" since 26 Sep 2026, with WG state "Candidate for WG Adoption". ([IETF datatracker, last updated 26 Sep 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-madhavan-aipref-displaybasedpref/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: an expired individual draft has no formal standing. Do not cite its proposed terms as an emerging standard.
- **[Amazon] Amazon documents three separately controllable user agents: `Amazonbot`, `Amzn-SearchBot` and `Amzn-User`.** `Amazonbot` "may be used to train Amazon AI models", `Amzn-SearchBot` serves search experiences such as Alexa and does not crawl for generative AI training, and `Amzn-User` fetches on a user's behalf and "may not follow all robots.txt directives". ([Amazon developer docs, last updated 26 Oct 2026](https://developer.amazon.com/amazonbot))
  - Why it matters: a site can now refuse Amazon's training crawler and still stay eligible for Alexa-style search. The page also treats the `noarchive` robots meta tag as "do not use the page for model training", so an existing `noarchive` already sends that signal to Amazon.
- **[UK CMA] The CMA's conduct requirement obliges Google to let publishers opt out of AI features in Google Search and of fine-tuning, and to attribute their content with clear links in AI-generated results.** Google has nine months to implement the changes and must publish compliance reports every six months for the first year. ([GOV.UK, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-fairer-deal-for-publishers-and-improves-google-search-services-in-uk)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: nine months from 3 Jun points to early March 2027 as the UK implementation date to watch. The compliance reports are the first scheduled public data on how Google meets the requirement.

### Google documentation

- **[Google] Google is testing Web Bot Auth with some AI clients on its infrastructure, signing a subset of `Google-Agent` requests as `https://agent.bot.goog`.** Google says it does not sign every request, so sites must fall back to IP, reverse-DNS and user-agent checks. ([Google crawling docs, last updated 13 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/web-bot-auth))
  - Why it matters: an unsigned request can still be a genuine Google request. A rule that blocks unsigned `Google-Agent` traffic would block real Google fetches.
- **[Google] Googlebot fetches only the first 2MB of an individual URL (PDFs excepted), headers included, and ignores the bytes after the cutoff.** PDFs have a 64MB limit, other crawlers that do not specify a limit default to 15MB, and referenced resources have their own per-URL byte counter. ([Google Search Central blog, 31 Mar 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/03/crawler-blog-post)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: content after the first 2MB of HTML is not fetched, rendered or indexed. Pages heavy with inline scripts or styles should carry their main text early in the document.
- **[Google] Google Search respects robots meta tags placed in the HTML body, not only in the head.** ([Google Search Central, last updated 24 Mar 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: a stray `noindex` or `nosnippet` in body markup, for example from a widget or template, takes effect. That includes the `nosnippet` that keeps a page out of AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- **[Google] Google lists three practices that make "Read more" deep links to a page section more likely.** Keep content immediately visible rather than behind expandable sections or tabs, do not set the scroll position with JavaScript on load, and do not remove the URL hash fragment on load. ([Google Search Central, last updated 20 Apr 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: deep links land readers on the exact section that answers the query. Content hidden in tabs or accordions lowers the chance of getting one.

### AI answer engines

- **[Microsoft Bing] A Bing post describes search ranking and grounding for AI answers as sharing a foundation but "optimized for fundamentally different outcomes".** In grounding, the unit of value is "groundable information" (discrete, supportable facts with clear provenance), and abstention is "a valid outcome when support is missing, stale, or conflicting". ([Microsoft Bing blog, 6 May 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/5/Evolving-role-of-the-index-From-ranking-pages-to-supporting-answers/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: this is Microsoft's own framing, not a measurement. Stated plainly, it says a page with stale or conflicting facts can leave the engine giving no answer at all instead of citing the page.
- **[Microsoft Bing] Microsoft launches Web IQ, a suite of grounding APIs built on the Bing index that connects AI systems to web data.** ([Microsoft Bing blog, 2 Jun 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/Announcing-Microsoft-Web-IQ/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: Bing's index is sold as grounding for third-party AI products. This post does not name ChatGPT or OpenAI, so it does not settle the "ChatGPT = Bing" question.
- **[Microsoft] Roadmap item 480725, SharePoint AI citations analytics, now reads "Launched".** The record was modified on 12 Oct 2026. ([Microsoft 365 roadmap API, modified 12 Oct 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/releasecommunications/api/v1/m365/480725))
  - Why it matters: citation analytics for SharePoint content move from planned to launched on Microsoft's roadmap. The roadmap record alone does not say which Copilot surfaces or tenants are covered.
- **[Microsoft Advertising] Microsoft's Publisher Content Marketplace pilot lets publishers set licensing terms for content used to ground specific Copilot answers, with usage-based reporting.** Microsoft says publishers "will be paid on delivered value", names co-design partners including The Associated Press, Condé Nast, Hearst Magazines and Vox Media, and is onboarding demand partners including Yahoo. ([Microsoft Advertising blog, 3 Feb 2026](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/february-2026/building-toward-a-sustainable-content-economy-for-the-agentic-web)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: this is a licensing model priced on use in answers, not on crawling. The payment terms are stated in the future tense, so the post is not evidence of payouts.
- **[xAI] xAI's tools API returns a `citations` list of every source the model encountered, and xAI says not every URL in it is referenced in the final answer.** Inline citations are on by default in the Responses API and off by default in the Python SDK, and even when on "the model decides when and where to include citations". ([xAI docs, page prints no date](https://docs.x.ai/developers/tools/citations.md))
  - Why it matters: a Grok-based tracker that counts the `citations` list overcounts citations. Only inline citations show what the answer actually cites.

### Industry studies

- **[Ahrefs — industry study] In 1.4 million ChatGPT 5.2 desktop prompts, URLs retrieved through search are cited at 88.46%, against 12.01% for news and 1.93% for Reddit.** The figures come from an internal retrieval field with five channels. ([Ahrefs, 15 Apr 2026, modified 27 Aug 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: being in ChatGPT's search results is close to a precondition for being cited. The page dates the prompts "February 2025" while naming ChatGPT 5.2, so the data window is uncertain.
- **[Semrush — industry study] In 3,981 domain appearances for 115 prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini and AI Mode, 61.7% are "ghost citations": the page is linked but the brand is not named.** 13.2% are both cited and mentioned, and 25.1% are mentions without a citation. ([Semrush, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: citation counts and brand-mention counts measure different things. A visibility report has to track both, and the prompt set behind this split is small.
- **[Seer Interactive — industry study] Organic CTR on queries with an AI Overview rose from 1.31% in December 2025 to 2.36% in February 2026, against 3.82% without one in February.** The data covers 53 brands, 5.47M tracked queries and 2.43B organic impressions, and AI Overview status is a label from the most recent SERP snapshot applied to all months. ([Seer Interactive, 24 Apr 2026](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: the CTR gap with AI Overviews narrows but stays large. The status label is applied backwards in time, so month-to-month changes mix SERP changes with click changes.
- **[Profound — industry study] Google AI Overviews cite social content for 20.0% of Spanish prompts from Spain and 22.5% from Mexico, against 15.3% across Profound's international dataset.** The study covers 3.25 billion citations from March 2026 across 7 models and 14 countries; for ChatGPT, social citation rates fall in every non-English market (Spain 4.24%). ([Profound, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/how-query-language-reshapes-ai-citations)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: for Spanish-language queries, community and social presence weighs more in AI Overviews than the English-language studies suggest. A Spanish market cannot be planned from English-language citation data.
- **[Similarweb — industry study] Similarweb's panel shows ChatGPT's share of generative-AI website visits falling from about 76% in June 2025 to about 53% by May 2026, while its total visits stay roughly flat.** Gemini rises from under 9% to about 27–28% over the same period; the metric is visits to AI platforms, not referrals from them. ([Similarweb, 29 Jul 2026, modified 17 Sep 2026](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/gen-ai-stats/)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: an engine mix weighted almost entirely to ChatGPT no longer matches where users are. The 73.3% and 56.7% figures flagged in W38 do not appear on this page, and the panel method is not described.
