## What's new in GEO

### Publisher controls & standards

- **[Cloudflare] The 15 Sep defaults did reach Googlebot — and Cloudflare shipped a way out on the same day.** This resolves the `needs-verification` item W38 left open, and it resolves it *both ways*. First, the bad half, in Cloudflare's own words: *"Block and Block on pages with ads now apply to mixed-use crawlers, including Applebot, Bingbot, and Googlebot, so either setting impacts search as well as training."* Second, the remedy: a new **Disallow AI Training** setting that *"Bot Preference Sync publishes … the applicable no-training preference in robots.txt"*, and which is *"named for the `Disallow:` directive it publishes in your robots.txt"*. Under it, *"Accountable mixed-use crawlers remain allowed for search. Every other training crawler is blocked."* Per-vendor, as Cloudflare states it: for Google the mechanism is a `Disallow` rule for **`Google-Extended`**, and *"Google has also stated that disallowing Google-Extended does not impact search ranking"*; for Apple it is **`Applebot-Extended`**; for Microsoft there is **no mechanism yet** — *"Microsoft is extending these capabilities and currently building the mechanism to also respect a 'no training' preference in robots.txt at the domain/site level, targeted for early 2027"*, so the setting *"will not automatically convey a no-training preference to Bing"*. New domains are preset by ad status: ad-supported sites default to **Disallow AI Training**, non-ad sites default to **Allow**. ([Cloudflare, 15 Sep 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/accountable-mixed-use-ai-crawlers/))
  - Why it matters: **this is the most consequential practitioner item of the week.** The warning the handbook carried — that choosing *Block* to stop training would also cost you Googlebot — is now confirmed by Cloudflare in plain text, and simultaneously obsolete as *advice*, because there is now a setting that expresses "no training, yes search" through the vendors' own robots.txt tokens instead of through an IP-layer block. If you are on Cloudflare and you picked **Block** or **Block on pages with ads** to keep your content out of training, you are blocking Googlebot for search too, today; **Disallow AI Training** is the setting you actually wanted. The asymmetry to plan around: it works for Google and Apple, and **does nothing for Bing until early 2027**. Chapter [04 · Technical GEO](../docs/04-technical.md#the-infrastructure-layer-block-by-default-and-pay-per-crawl) updated with a dated correction.
- **[llms.txt] The first server-log measurement lands with a DOI — and its headline is that the file is *found*, not *used*.** Nathan Hall, *"Discovery, not adoption: crawler retrieval of an `llms.txt` resource announced through `robots.txt`, across seven sites on one server, and what a server log can and cannot show"*, preprint, **17 Sep 2026**, CC BY 4.0 ([10.5281/zenodo.22814844](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22814844)). Across six WordPress sites and **165 announced site-days**, crawlers requested `/ai/llms.txt` **1,549 times, 9.4 per site-day (95% interval 8.9 to 9.9)** — but only because the URL was named in a `robots.txt` `Sitemap:` line. The controls are what make it worth reading: *"Ten un-referenced files in the same directory received zero requests across 60 file-months"*; one sibling referenced only by an HTML `link` tag got **13, all from OpenAI crawlers**; the **un-announced root `/llms.txt`, present on all seven sites, was requested twice**; and a seventh site carrying only a root file logged **373 passing crawler requests and none for `llms.txt`**. **ClaudeBot** alone accounted for **1,131** requests, a median **8 seconds** after its most recent same-site `robots.txt` request, and fetched the site's XML sitemap at the same rate. The author's own conclusion is the quotable one: *"Tell a crawler where a file is and it fetches the file. That is discovery, not adoption."* He is explicit about the ceiling: the observations *"do not establish recognition of llms.txt, parsing, indexing, ranking, or use in AI-generated answers."* `⚠️ Declared competing interest, in the deposit's own words: "the author owns Lefty Media Co and builds the plugin that announces the file measured here."`
  - Why it matters: the handbook's [standing verdict](../docs/04-technical.md#llmstxt-the-emerging-and-unproven-convention) — no engine confirms reading `llms.txt` for retrieval or citation — **survives, and now has a measurement behind it instead of only an absence of announcements.** The practical upgrade is the confound: raw hit counts on `/llms.txt` are close to worthless as evidence of engine interest. Chapter [04 · Technical GEO](../docs/04-technical.md#llmstxt-the-emerging-and-unproven-convention) updated.
- **[Google] John Mueller supplies the caveat to that study himself, in public.** Replying to the author on **17 Sep 2026**, Google's Mueller wrote: *"I think this kind of thing is only testable like this 'at the moment'. There are a bunch of 'everything llms.txt' sites out there now that try to check all domains for the llms.txt file and link to it from their site. So when a crawler sees their site, they see the links, and follow them."* The author agreed the next day and gave the split: the root `/llms.txt` *"got 2 verified crawler fetches in 31 days and 565 from scanners and SEO tools.   - Why it matters: **565 of the root file's fetches were scanners and SEO tools, against 2 verified crawler fetches.** Anyone reporting "AI engines are hitting my `llms.txt`" from a raw access log is, on this evidence, mostly counting the directory sites and audit tools that exist *because* of the `llms.txt` discourse. Verify the user-agent, or the metric measures the hype rather than the engines.

### Research

- **[arXiv] HAE-GEO: a benchmark for what an agent does *after* it swallows poisoned content.** *"Evaluating Deep-Search Agents under Hierarchical Web Evidence Poisoning"*, Bi et al., **v2 dated 17 Sep 2026** (v1 5 Sep) — [arXiv:2609.06027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06027). The framing, verbatim: *"Existing benchmarks largely measure whether manipulated content is retrieved or endorsed, but do not track whether an agent verifies suspicious evidence, revises adopted claims, or recovers before producing its final recommendation."* Scale: **10 agents**, **72,039 clean pages** and **770 poisoned pages per level**, three escalating attack levels, **8 product categories**. Findings, verbatim: *"evidence recognition degrades under the corroboration trap; agentic search improves final resistance without improving evidence recognition or utility; and defense prompting increases verification, yet rarely converts verification into recovery."*
  - Why it matters: this is **GEO's adversarial edge measured as a trajectory** rather than a single hit/miss — and the "corroboration trap" result matters for legitimate practitioners too, because it says agents weigh *agreement across sources* more than *quality of sources*. That is a mechanism, not a trick, and it belongs in the honest discussion of where the line sits. Chapters [07 · Research & Cases](../docs/07-research-cases.md#an-honest-note-on-the-state-of-the-literature) and [08 · Future & Ethics](../docs/08-future-ethics.md) updated.
- **[arXiv] *Settled from W38* — the "Caption Injection" paper is real.** W38 flagged *Caption Injection for Optimization in Generative Search Engine* (Chen et al., reported as ECML PKDD) as a search-only lead with no primary page. It exists: **[arXiv:2511.04080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04080)**, Xiaolu Chen, Jie Bao, Haojie Wu, Zhen Chen and Yong Liao, v1 6 Nov 2025 through **v4 29 Jun 2026**, marked **ECML PKDD 2026 accepted**. It proposes *"Caption Injection, the first multimodal G-SEO approach"* — extracting image captions and injecting them into text — and reports that it *"significantly outperforms text-only G-SEO baselines"* under G-EVAL on the MRAMG benchmark. `⚠️ Why W38 missed it: the arXiv ID is a 2025 one, so it never appeared in the 2026 GEO listings that were checked.   - Why it matters: procedurally more than substantively — **"not in this year's listing" is not "does not exist"**, and a title search against the full arXiv API settles in seconds what a year-scoped listing sweep cannot.
