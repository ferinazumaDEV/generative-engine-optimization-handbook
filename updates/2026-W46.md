## What's new in GEO

### Publisher controls & standards

- **[Google] Google says it aims to support, together with other AI providers, a shared robots.txt "policy token" for AI-training preferences, built on the IETF AIPREF working group's work, in 2027.** When a site opts out of training with this token and does not mention `Google-Extended`, Google plans to treat that as if the site had disallowed `Google-Extended`. Existing company-specific controls keep working alongside the shared signal. ([Google Search Central blog, 30 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/10/ai-training-control))
  - Why it matters: Google's crawler docs name the token as `user-agent: __usage_ai-training`, with support intended in early 2027 ([Google common crawlers page, last updated 30 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers)). The same post says that sites which specifically allow `Google-Extended` are eligible for grounding in Gemini Apps. A training opt-out through the shared token, with no `Google-Extended` rule, would be read as a `Google-Extended` disallow under the plan, so it could also cost that eligibility.
- **[IETF AIPREF] An individual Internet-Draft, `draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal-00`, describes how to express usage preferences such as "no AI training" in ordinary robots.txt groups keyed to a per-purpose policy token in the `User-agent` line.** It adds no new robots.txt directives or grammar. It is an individual draft, not a working-group document or an RFC. ([IETF datatracker, 30 Oct 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/))
  - Why it matters: this is the kind of syntax Google's announcement describes. Until it is adopted and stable, treat the exact token form as provisional.
- **[Google] Google's robots.txt specification now recommends percent-encoding the paths in `Allow` and `Disallow` rules, so the file is easier for third-party tools and services to process.** ([Google crawling docs changelog, entry 23 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/changelog); [Google robots.txt specification, last updated 23 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec))
  - Why it matters: many non-Google AI crawlers parse the same robots.txt file. Encoding paths consistently reduces the chance that different parsers read a rule differently.
- **[Cloudflare] Cloudflare renames its AI bot setting to "AI Crawler and Agent access policies" and lets site owners set separate actions for supported personal AI agents: Allow, Block on pages with ads, or Block.** These sit apart from the Search and Training policies, and Security Analytics adds a "Personal Assistants" traffic category. ([Cloudflare blog, 4 Nov 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/personal-agents-visibility-control/))
  - Why it matters: a major CDN now treats agents acting for a user as their own traffic class, separate from training and search crawlers. Sites behind Cloudflare should check which policy covers each kind of AI traffic.
- **[Cloudflare] Cloudflare announces a Trusted Agent Delegation (TAD) coalition to define the problems of agents acting for users through intermediaries and to bring that work to the IETF, with Web Bot Auth as its preferred way for agents to identify themselves.** ([Cloudflare blog, 5 Nov 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/trusted-agent-delegation/))
  - Why it matters: standards work is moving beyond identifying crawlers to identifying agents and who they act for. This is an announcement, not a standard.
- **[IETF webbotauth] Revision `-01` of the working group's protocol draft, `draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol`, changes how bots identify their keys.** Every `Signature-Agent` value is now a direct JWK Set URI. It recommends that origins expecting a signature request one with `Accept-Signature`, and it says crawlers and user-directed agents should use distinct `Signature-Agent` URLs. It is still a work in progress, not an RFC. ([IETF datatracker, 29 Oct 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol/))
  - Why it matters: this updates the `-00` revision logged in W45. Code that verifies signed bots against `-00` needs revisiting. Distinct URLs would let a site tell a crawler from an agent acting for a user.
- **[IETF webbotauth] Three individual drafts in the same area appear in the window.** They are all individual drafts, not adopted by the working group:
  - `draft-vilgalys-webbotauth-signed-agent-metadata-00` proposes an optional signed header with pseudonymous product, project and session identifiers, so that verifiers can rate-limit tenants of a shared agent platform one by one ([IETF datatracker, 2 Nov 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-vilgalys-webbotauth-signed-agent-metadata/)).
  - `draft-kelley-webbotauth-sail-00` defines SAIL (Source Address Identity List), a metadata format served at a well-known URI ([IETF datatracker, 22 Oct 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-webbotauth-sail/)).
  - `draft-meunier-webbotauth-registry-04` updates a registry and a JSON "Signature Agent card" that signing bots publish to describe themselves ([IETF datatracker, 27 Oct 2026](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-meunier-webbotauth-registry/)).
  - Why it matters: vendor IP lists and user-agent strings may give way to published, machine-readable bot identities. None of these drafts is a commitment yet.

### AI answer engines

- **[Microsoft Bing] Bing Webmaster Tools adds Topics Portfolio (beta) to its AI Performance report.** It groups the grounding queries where a site is cited into topic clusters. For each topic it shows citation demand, the number of cited sites, distinct query counts, and "opportunity" queries where other sites are cited and this one is not. Microsoft says the view rests on AI-generated topic classification and asks that the numbers be read as directional rather than exact. ([Bing Webmaster Blog, 13 Oct 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/10/introducing-topics-portfolio-beta-in-ai-performance/))
  - Why it matters: this is an engine-side view of which topics a site is cited for in Bing's AI Search grounding. It can serve as a baseline next to third-party trackers, read as directional figures.

### Research

- **[arXiv preprint] LIVE-GEO publishes GEO-rewritten product pages for 80 fictional brands on the live web and tests five GEO strategies on three generative engines.** The engines pick the same top product for only 48.8%–66.3% of brands, and ChatGPT's results change with the order of the candidate URLs. ([Kong et al., arXiv:2611.01126, 31 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01126))
  - Why it matters: gains measured in fixed-context benchmarks may not carry over to live engines, and the order of candidates is a confound that audits must control for.
- **[arXiv preprint] A paper compares rerankers and decision models for predicting which candidate pages ten answer engines cite under controlled retrieval.** It releases a fine-tuned 4B reranker ("GEO Citation Ranker 4B") with the benchmark and code. ([Bajemon & Rochet, arXiv:2610.38090, 28 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38090))
  - Why it matters: it offers an open, reproducible baseline for estimating citation likelihood before measuring live engines. It is a model of controlled retrieval, not of production engines.
- **[arXiv preprint] An audit of 300 questions finds that ChatGPT's cited sources follow the location the application declares, down to city level, and ignore the network location, while Gemini follows the network's country and ignores the declared location.** The businesses each engine recommends shift with those sources. ([Chen & Koudas, arXiv:2610.24081, 16 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24081))
  - Why it matters: citation-visibility measurements depend on location. An audit must fix and report both the declared location and the network location.
- **[arXiv preprint] A paper introduces "Implicit Brand Injection", a multi-agent method that inserts a single, contextually blended brand mention into an article, and IBIBench (1,000 articles, 60 synthetic brands, 12 domains).** It reports higher retrieval exposure and brand visibility in generated answers. ([Kriplani & Wang, arXiv:2611.04005, 2 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04005))
  - Why it matters: it documents an adversarial technique aimed at generated answers. It is relevant to detecting manipulation, and it is not a recommended practice.
- **[arXiv preprint] A paper argues that GEO benchmarks mix up algorithmic gains with differences in how freely each method may edit the source.** It proposes a common evaluation standard: a meta-taxonomy of where, how and which signals are edited, plus a controlled-comparison protocol with explicit coverage reporting. ([Wen et al., arXiv:2610.23541, 16 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23541))
  - Why it matters: it gives a vocabulary for checking whether reported GEO uplifts are comparable across studies.

### Industry studies

- **[Ahrefs — industry study] Ahrefs asked the same questions every day for 20 days across AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, tracking about 37,500 citations.** Depending on the engine, 50–83% of citations were gone the next day, about 75% of dropped citations returned within 20 days, and 64% of Gemini answers and 53% of Copilot answers had no citations. Ahrefs states the caveats: the page-characteristic findings are correlations, the ranking analysis covered only 230 questions, return rates may be underestimated near the end of the window, and only the first disappearance was tracked. ([Ahrefs, 29 Oct 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-stable-are-ai-citations/))
  - Why it matters: a single check of AI visibility is unreliable, and measurement needs repeated sampling over days.
- **[Ahrefs — industry study] Across 10,000 US brands and about 260M non-branded prompts on ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews, Ahrefs finds Spearman correlations of 0.59–0.75 between off-site mention types and brand mentions in AI answers.** Reddit has the highest correlation (about 0.75) and TikTok the lowest (0.60). Ahrefs states the caveats: correlation only, exact-phrase brand matching, and Reddit's licensing deals as a possible partial explanation. The data comes from Ahrefs' own Brand Radar and Content Explorer. ([Ahrefs, 3 Nov 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/mention-types-ai-visibility/))
  - Why it matters: it shows which off-site sources move together with AI visibility. It does not show that building mentions causes visibility.
- **[Ahrefs — industry study] In a 5.5M-answer index of US ChatGPT answers, the most-cited of 85 OpenAI partner domains are mainly review and lifestyle sites, not news sites.** In a separate query fan-out study cited in the post, partner sites among ChatGPT's 200 most-cited domains account for 7.5% of its citations in March 2026, 15.8% in July and 12.5% in late September. Ahrefs states the caveats: a single snapshot, and US-only data, so publishers writing mainly in other languages are undercounted. ([Ahrefs, 3 Nov 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/openai-publisher-partnerships/))
  - Why it matters: it puts a number on how licensing deals relate to ChatGPT citation share, within the stated sampling limits.
