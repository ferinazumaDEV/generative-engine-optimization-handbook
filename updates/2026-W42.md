## What's new in GEO

### Publisher controls & standards

- **[Cloudflare] Cloudflare is testing `content-use`, a fourth Content Signals field.** The optional extension adds `use=immediate | reference | full` (least to most permissive) next to `search`, `ai-input` and `ai-train`, and for customers who turned on managed robots.txt Cloudflare adds `use=reference`, so the managed file emits `Content-signal: search=yes, ai-train=no, use=reference`. ([Cloudflare docs, last updated 8 Oct 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/managed-robots-txt/))
  - Why it matters: sites on managed robots.txt now publish a new usage preference they did not write themselves. Read your served `robots.txt` before you assume what it says. The page does not say what the 8 Oct edit changed.
- **[Cloudflare] Pay Per Use enters beta: AI buyers price each defined use, and publishers accept or decline.** Buyers self-report each use, and Cloudflare bills them and pays publishers monthly. ([Cloudflare, 30 Sep 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/pay-per-use/))
  - Why it matters: this licensing model pays for use, not for crawl. The payment rests on the buyer's own usage reports, so a publisher has no independent count to check against.
- **[Cloudflare] A Web Search API launches through AI Gateway, with Ceramic.ai, Exa and Linkup as launch partners.** Cloudflare says each partner's crawler must meet its "Verified bots" requirements and each search response must link to the location of the crawled content. ([Cloudflare, 2 Oct 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-web-search-api/))
  - Why it matters: these are three more retrieval providers that can put your pages in front of AI answers. Their crawlers are tied to Cloudflare's verified-bot rules, so allowlists and log filters need their names.

### Google documentation

- **[Google] Search Console adds a "multimodal search" type filter, including in the Generative AI features report.** It covers Lens, Circle to Search, image uploads to Google Search and Chrome's "Search this image", and it is rolling out globally. ([Google Search Central blog, 24 Sep 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc))
  - Why it matters: image-led queries can now be separated from text queries in the same report that shows AI-feature performance. A series that spans 24 Sep should state whether it uses the filter.
- **[Google] The guide on using generative AI content calls it "critical" to manually fact-check and review all AI-generated content before publishing.** The review covers titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text, and the page points to rater-guideline sections 4.6.5 (scaled content abuse) and 4.6.6 (little effort, originality or added value). ([Google Search Central, last updated 1 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content))
  - Why it matters: the review duty reaches markup and metadata, not only body copy. The stamp shows the page changed on 1 Oct, but the page does not say which passages are new.
- **[Google] Google's AI optimization guide says `llms.txt` and other AI text files "will neither harm nor help" visibility in Google Search, because Google Search ignores them.** It also says content does not need chunking or a special writing style for generative AI search. ([Google Search Central, last updated 10 Jul 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: this is Google's plainest first-party statement on `llms.txt`. Its scope is Google Search only; the page leaves the files open for other services.
- **[Google] Google documents `Google-Agent` for actions that Google-hosted agents take at a user's request, and `Google-GeminiNotebook` as the renamed notebook fetcher.** The page says user-triggered fetchers "generally ignore robots.txt rules", lists `Google-NotebookLM` as supported until August 2026, and publishes IP ranges in `user-triggered-agents.json`. ([Google crawling docs, last updated 19 Aug 2026](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-user-triggered-fetchers)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: Google says these user-triggered fetchers generally ignore robots.txt rules, so a robots.txt rule cannot be relied on to stop them. A rule still written for `Google-NotebookLM` names an agent Google no longer documents as current.

### AI answer engines

- **[Microsoft] The roadmap now shows General Availability for deep citations in Copilot in February CY2027.** On 12 Oct 2026, roadmap item 523223 reads status "In development", GA February CY2027, public preview January CY2026, last modified 30 Sep 2026. ([Microsoft 365 roadmap API, modified 30 Sep 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/releasecommunications/api/v1/m365/523223))
  - Why it matters: the W39 entry recorded different values (GA November CY2026, public preview October CY2026, modified 16 Sep). Do not plan Copilot citation-depth measurement around a 2026 date. The API returns current values only, so the change can be seen only against that earlier entry.
- **[Perplexity] Sonar Chat Completions support ended on 27 Sep 2026, but synchronous and streaming Sonar requests still work, because Perplexity reformulates them as Agent API requests.** The rollout is gradual by model. Asynchronous Sonar requests are no longer supported. ([Perplexity migration guide, page prints no date](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/agent-api/migrate-from-sonar/overview.md))
  - Why it matters: a tracker that kept calling Sonar after the deadline may still get answers, but those answers now come from a different pipeline. Treat 27 Sep as a break in any Perplexity citation series.
- **[Perplexity] Agent API presets switched models in September and moved to Fast Search in October.** The September changes move `low`/`medium` to `openai/gpt-6-luna`, `high` to `openai/gpt-6-sol` and `xhigh` to an Anthropic model. A later October entry moves `low`, `medium` and `high` to Fast Search, which leaves only `xhigh` on standard web search. ([Perplexity changelog, entries labelled September and October 2026](https://docs.perplexity.ai/changelog.md))
  - Why it matters: callers on dynamic presets pick up these changes with no change to their requests. This is a vendor-side confound for any series built on presets; frozen preset configurations are not affected.
- **[Google Ads] Ads inside AI Overviews run in English in 12 countries, and advertisers cannot opt out of the placement.** The countries are Australia, Canada, India, Indonesia, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, Singapore and the US; the placement gets no segmented reporting and excludes sensitive verticals. ([Google Ads Help, page prints no date](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en))
  - Why it matters: paid units sit inside the answer block in these 12 markets. A citation count that reads the whole AI Overview must exclude them, and the advertiser's reports cannot separate them out.
- **[OpenAI] ChatGPT "offline web search" for eligible workspaces uses "OpenAI's indexed and cached web content" instead of live web search.** The eligible workspaces are certain Enterprise, Edu, Healthcare, Teachers, regulated and federal configurations. ([OpenAI Help Center, page shows "Updated: 2 months ago" on 12 Oct 2026](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001203-offline-web-search-for-chatgpt-workspaces))
  - Why it matters: this is first-party evidence that OpenAI holds its own index or cache of web pages. It does not say how that index is built or which provider serves live search, so the "ChatGPT = Bing" question stays open.
- **[Legal — news report] Judge Amit Mehta dismissed Penske Media's Sherman Act suit against Google over AI Overviews, and Chegg's similar complaint, because no "formal bargain" was pleaded.** Mehta wrote: "But an expectation is not an agreement. It is simply how a general search engine works." ([Press Gazette, 1 Oct 2026, updated 2 Oct](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/penske-ai-overviews-lawsuit-dismissed-because-no-formal-bargain-struck-with-google/))
  - Why it matters: the court treated the traffic-for-content exchange as an expectation, not an agreement. For now, US antitrust law gives publishers no implied contract with Google over AI Overviews.

### Industry studies

- **[Cloudflare — industry study] Cloudflare's network data shows that fewer than 1% of sites on Cloudflare block search crawlers while 17% block training crawlers.** It also reports that AI training's share of crawler requests, classified by the crawlers' stated purpose, rose from 22% in Spring 2025 to 52% by June 2026, and that daily AI-agent requests grew by more than 1,700% in a year. ([Cloudflare, 30 Sep 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-web/))
  - Why it matters: most sites refuse training while staying open to search, the split that the 15 Sep Disallow AI Training setting expresses. This is vendor telemetry from a company that sells bot controls, and the post gives no per-metric sample or method.

### Research

- **[arXiv — industry study, vendor-authored preprint] In about 2 million citations from four AI answer engines, the usual AEO checklist (FAQ blocks, structured data, Core Web Vitals) shows positive effects in pooled data that reverse or collapse to zero under domain fixed effects.** Prompt-content alignment is the dominant page-level predictor (β = +0.37), and the page sample is 10,000 pages from nineteen B2B SaaS workspaces. ([arXiv:2609.35077, 28 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35077))
  - Why it matters: checklist effects measured across domains may simply be domain effects. Both authors work at a company (Discovered Labs), and the paper is not peer-reviewed.
- **[arXiv — preprint] In a randomized field experiment with 37,561 Washington Post readers, AI answers with article citations above the results moved readers from conventional-result clicks toward cited articles and follow-up searches.** Article consumption per reader rose slightly and spread to less-popular topics. ([arXiv:2609.38946, 30 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38946))
  - Why it matters: this is causal evidence from inside one publisher's own site search that a cited answer can redirect clicks to the cited articles rather than replace them. It is not evidence about Google's AI Overviews.
- **[AIES 2026 — peer-reviewed] An audit of ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity on 712 queries about politics, health and the environment finds evidence of AI-generated sources among the citations on all four engines, at about 16% of cited sources.** ([arXiv:2605.23684, 22 May 2026, journal-ref AIES 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23684)) *Missed earlier.*
  - Why it matters: being cited alongside generated content is now a measured condition, not a hypothetical one. The 16% figure is approximate and depends on a detector.

### Prompting

- **[OpenAI] OpenAI's "Using GPT-6" guide tells developers to remove `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_logprobs` whenever reasoning effort is not `none`.** The instruction applies to `gpt-6-astra`, `gpt-6.1-sol` and `gpt-6-luna`. ([OpenAI API docs, page prints no date](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model))
  - Why it matters: a measurement harness that fixes temperature for repeatability cannot do so on these models at non-zero reasoning effort. Repeat-run variance has to be measured, not configured away.

### Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)

- **[Google Workspace] In Google Docs, users on eligible plans can reference an existing Gemini Notebook with "@", and Gemini then grounds its Docs output in that notebook's sources with inline citations.** ([Google Workspace Updates, 23 Sep 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/09/ground-ai-prompts-in-google-docs-on-existing-sources-from-Gemini-Notebook.html))
  - Why it matters: a notebook's source list now reaches Docs output as citations. Being a source in someone's notebook is becoming a citation surface of its own.
