### AI answer engines

- **[Google] "People also ask" answers are now almost entirely AI Overviews.** Two vendor datasets converge. AlsoAsked, working from *"roughly 19.2 million English-language queries logged during 2026"*, puts AI-generated PAA answers at **86% in August and 97% in the first week of September 2026** — against about **12% fourteen months earlier** — and adds *"It seems highly likely we are on track for 100% shortly"*; Allintitle's *Also Ask Miner* records **100% since August**. ([Search Engine Roundtable, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/ppa-ai-overviews-google-42047.html), relaying both vendors' LinkedIn posts · [PPC Land, 10 Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/publishers-spent-113m-in-one-month-buying-back-their-search-traffic/)) `⚠️ vendor data; the primary LinkedIn posts were not retrieved.`
  - Why it matters: the PAA box was a separate citation surface with its own per-question attribution. Those answers are now AI Overviews, so their citations live in the AI Overview panel — and Google was simultaneously testing a **thinner AI Overview citations panel** on the right-hand side ([Search Engine Roundtable, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-thinner-ai-overview-citations-panel-42040.html), replicated by the author, not confirmed by Google) and **paginated follow-up responses with a skip button in AI Mode** ([Search Engine Roundtable, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-mode-paginated-responses-skip-42029.html)). Net effect on a results page: fewer, smaller link surfaces. Chapter [02](../docs/02-engines.md#google--ai-overviews-ai-mode--gemini) updated.
- **[Google] *Missed in W37* — AI Mode on Gemini 3.8 Flash shipped without citations for about a day.** Right after the model reached AI Mode (2 Sep), Gagan Ghotra and Glenn Gabe posted top-of-funnel queries answered **with no links or sources at all**. Google's Robby Stein replied *"This isn't working as intended, and we'll roll out a fix soon"*, and links were back the following morning (4 Sep). ([Search Engine Land, 3 Sep 2026](https://searchengineland.com/google-to-fix-citation-bug-with-gemini-3-8-flash-in-ai-mode-486892) · [Search Engine Roundtable, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-mode-gemini-38-no-links-42011.html))
  - Why it matters: citation display is a **per-model property** and can regress silently. Any citation-measurement run must record which model served the answer, or a day of zeros gets read as a visibility loss. Chapter [02](../docs/02-engines.md#google--ai-overviews-ai-mode--gemini) updated.
- **[Google] Merchant Center now shows the *language* of AI shopping queries.** Google's *AI performance insights* for Merchant Center cover **AI Mode and AI Overviews** and add **Top search intents** (*"the context behind conversational queries, moving beyond keywords to reveal the underlying shopper intent"*), **Top terms** (*"search terms within a product category that shoppers are actively prioritizing in their conversational queries"*) and **Popular attributes** (*"structured technical specifications customers look for, such as Size, Color, or Material, that may be missing from your product data sources"*). ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17200695?hl=en) · rollout noticed by Brodie Clark, [Search Engine Roundtable, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-merchant-center-ai-performance-new-42043.html))
  - Why it matters: Search Console's AI performance report still exposes **no query terms**; this is the first first-party Google surface that shows *what people asked* an AI feature — product-only and feed-level, but real. Chapter [06 · Measurement](../docs/06-measurement.md#google-search-console-the-ai-performance-report) updated.
- **[OpenAI] ChatGPT for Financial Services (10 Sep 2026): the second vertical in ten days answered from licensed data, not ranked pages.** The product *"combines OpenAI's latest GPT-6 Astra model with embedded institutional content from premier financial data providers, including PitchBook, Daloopa, and LSEG News"*, with *"granular, line-item citations"* that trace figures back to the *"original source document, earnings call transcript, or financial statement."* ([Yahoo Finance, 10 Sep 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-launches-chatgpt-financial-services-204024031.html), syndicated coverage) `⚠️ openai.com and cnbc.com returned HTTP 403 to automated fetchers, so the primary post at openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-financial-services/ was not retrieved.`
  - Why it matters: last week it was healthcare (Epic and official public databases, 1 Sep); this week finance. For whole verticals ChatGPT is being pointed at **licensed structured connectors instead of the open web**, which caps how much open-web content in that vertical can be cited at all. Law is the obvious next candidate. Chapter [02](../docs/02-engines.md#chatgpt--chatgpt-search-openai) updated.
- **[OpenAI / Amazon] Amazon DSP starts selling ads inside ChatGPT (10 Sep 2026).** A US pilot lets *"select advertisers in the U.S."* buy ChatGPT placements through Amazon DSP, with Delta Vacations *"among the first brands"*; the units run *"as text and image units beneath organic ChatGPT responses"* on a CPC or CPM basis, and *"OpenAI will continue to control ad delivery and placement through its own systems."* ([Amazon Ads, 10 Sep 2026](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/news/amazon-ads-chat-gpt-advertising-integration) · [Search Engine Land, 10 Sep 2026](https://searchengineland.com/amazon-pilots-chatgpt-ads-through-its-dsp-488026))
  - Why it matters: a third-party demand source now buys against ChatGPT answers. OpenAI's standing claim that ads are separate from and do not influence answers (recorded in [W37](2026-W37.md)) is now load-bearing for Amazon's advertisers too. Organic GEO stays a separate discipline; the separation should be tested, not assumed.
- **[Perplexity] The Sonar retirement holds at 27 Sep 2026 — reconfirmed by staff on 10 Sep.** Asked whether the old endpoint survives past the date, Perplexity's Andrew Madson replied the same day: *"The Sonar chat completions endpoint will retire on September 27. You will need to migrate to the Agent API responses endpoint before then."* The developer changelog, fetched 14 Sep, still carries no entry about the retirement; its September entries are custom MCP connectors, OAuth for the remote MCP server, and Gemini 3.8 Flash on the Agent API. ([Perplexity API forum, 10 Sep 2026](https://community.perplexity.ai/t/sonar-moving-to-agents-api/6061) · [docs changelog](https://docs.perplexity.ai/changelog) · [migration guide](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/agent-api/migrate-from-sonar/overview))
  - Why it matters: **thirteen days left** for any citation-tracking pipeline on `sonar`/`sonar-pro`. Migrate and re-baseline — a different endpoint is not guaranteed to retrieve or cite identically. Chapters [02](../docs/02-engines.md#perplexity) and [06](../docs/06-measurement.md) updated.
- **[Perplexity] Q2D-Web: a vendor retrieval benchmark that uses agent citations as relevance labels.** *Q2D-Web: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Retrieval in Agentic RAG Systems* ([arXiv:2609.08887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08887), submitted 8 Sep 2026; announced on Perplexity's forum on 9 Sep) pairs a **190M-document web corpus** with **70k agentic search queries in ten languages, "reformulated from real-world user queries in production systems"**, and provides *"three sets of fixed relevance judgments: agent citations, production rankings, and a combined set."* ([announcement](https://community.perplexity.ai/t/q2d-web-evaluating-first-stage-retrievers-at-scale/6052); Perplexity's blog post returned HTTP 403, so the arXiv abstract is cited)
  - Why it matters: two things a practitioner can take from the abstract alone — the first-stage retriever serves **machine-rewritten queries, not the user's words**, and **what the agent cited is treated as a ground-truth signal**. It is a public corpus on which "retrieved" and "cited" can be told apart. Preprint; vendor-released.

### Research & data

- **[Preprint] A content-only score barely predicts citation, and the 2023 GEO levers no longer move it.** *Scoring Without the Engine: Validating a Deterministic, Manipulation-Resistant Content Score for Generative Engines, End to End* (Bajemon & Rochet, [arXiv:2609.07559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07559), 7 Sep 2026) validates a cheap content-quality proxy with adversarial falsification gates across *"ten modern engine families"* and a *"500-source benchmark of adversarial edits."* The figures: amplifying the score's levers *"gains an attacker at most 6 points"*; citation prediction reaches only *"within-query Spearman 0.11"*; and *"re-measuring the only published causal anchors (2023 effect sizes) on ten modern engine families shows their levers move citation on none."* `⚠️ vendor-affiliated — the comments field links Citead.com; code and data are published; not peer-reviewed, not replicated.`
  - Why it matters: it is in-window evidence for what chapter 07 already warns — the founding paper's magnitudes are era-specific — and a reminder that content scoring without measuring the engine is a weak proxy. Chapter [07](../docs/07-research-cases.md#an-honest-note-on-the-state-of-the-literature) logs it with caveats.
- **[Preprint] A causal attribution framework for GEO and paid AI placements — simulation only.** *Generative Marketing Mix Modeling: A Causal Inference Framework Linking GEO and GEM to Business Impact* (Kato, Honma & Kato, [arXiv:2609.11915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11915), 10 Sep 2026) puts organic presence in generated answers and sponsored placements in the same causal ledger as classic channels, and states identification conditions. Validation uses *"simulated answers to product recommendation in English and Japanese"* — no empirical citation, traffic or revenue figures.
  - Why it matters: a method to borrow, not a data point to cite.
- **[Vendor study] Semrush (8 Sep 2026): in manufacturing, AI sends 0.48% of sessions, and the brands AI *mentions* are not the sites it *cites*.** From US clickstream data across ten manufacturing categories, Jan–Jul 2026, *"just 0.48% of all sessions to manufacturing sites originate from AI search tools or assistants"*; across 458 tracked keywords AI Overviews grew from **38% to 57%** of search volume; and *"of the 15 top domains by each metric, only two appear on both the 'most-mentioned brands' and 'most-cited sources' lists"* (Vevor and Grainger) — 3M, John Deere and Boeing lead mentions and are absent from the cited lists. ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/manufacturing-seo-ai-search/), vendor study · [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/only-0-48-of-manufacturing-site-traffic-comes-from-ai-semrush-finds/))
  - Why it matters: mentions and citations are different quantities measured in different places, exactly the distinction [06 · Measurement](../docs/06-measurement.md) insists on. One vertical, one vendor — do not generalise the 0.48%.
- **[Publisher data] Le Monde says AI Overviews have not dented its audience — with numbers.** Group CEO Louis Dreyfus, in [INfluencia, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.influencia.net/louis-dreyfus-nous-navons-pas-constate-dimpact-dai-overviews-sur-nos-audiences/): *"Nous n'avons pas constaté de baisse soudaine de nos audiences."* Figures he gives: **24,000 new subscribers in each of July and August 2026**, apps at **36% of group traffic** in August, the English edition **+7%** in H1 2026 vs H1 2025, and readers arriving from ChatGPT links converting to subscribers at **26× the rate of Facebook referrals** and **260× Google Discover**. (English summary: [Search Engine Journal, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/le-monde-says-ai-overviews-havent-hurt-its-audience-so-far/589018/)) `⚠️ publisher's own figures, unaudited; subscription-led model.`
  - Why it matters: one counter-example to the traffic-collapse narrative, and one of the very few public conversion comparisons for AI referrals. The 26× is a ratio against Facebook, not an absolute rate.
- **[Market data] Publishers are buying back search traffic: $113M in paid search in one month.** Adweek, from Similarweb's Top 100 Media index: the hundred largest publishers *"spent an estimated $113 million on paid search"* in July 2026, *"up 41% year over year and 274% over the past three years"*, for 23.7 million paid visits (+148% over three years); Forbes alone **$72.2M** (organic traffic **−26.7%** YoY), CNN organic **−28.9%**, USA Today **−24.1%**. ([Adweek, Sep 2026](https://www.adweek.com/media/publishers-buying-search-traffic/); Mediagazer summary dated 10 Sep 2026) `⚠️ Similarweb estimates, not audited spend.`
  - Why it matters: the price of substituted organic traffic, in dollars. Spend is rising faster than the visits it buys.
